    The framework, in brief

    Inner periphery — irreducibly human

    Functions that depend on embodied presence and the capacity for authentic correction. Not delegable to AI.

    • Formal meditation practice
    • Dharma and contemplative transmission
    • Course correction
    • Crisis attunement
    • Sangha as shared vulnerability
    • Embodied silence as contemplative technology

    Boundary zone — practitioner-governed

    AI may operate here only under teacher judgment, subject to three non-negotiable conditions.

    • Teacher threshold (6–12 months). Students eventually reach states only a human teacher can navigate.
    • Crisis threshold. Suicidality, psychosis, and acute trauma always require human intervention.
    • Containment principle. AI draws only on practitioner-approved content — vault-only sourcing.

    Outer periphery — AI-assisted under governance

    Functions AI may support, with a practitioner accountable for the result.

    • Knowledge synthesis, curriculum design, practice scaffolding
    • Data and self-tracking, teacher archives, wearable biometric integration
    • Access and community extension, trauma-informed adaptation

    The sycophancy–delusion loop

    The framework's central design failure: AI that validates without challenging. Lost struggle produces flabby mindfulness; frictionless substitution erodes motivation for harder human relationships; commercial incentives reward both. Tools should de-center the self and leave users more connected to others, not less.

    Haberlin, S. Governing AI at the clinical threshold: a grounded theory framework for responsible AI deployment in mindfulness-based mental health. AI & Society. doi.org/10.1007/s00146-026-03341-x
